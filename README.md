# Digital-Divide-Project

An exploratory data analysis project investigating  educational technology spending and internet accessibility across public schools in the United States.

# Overview 

The digital divide continues to affect millions of students across the United States by limiting access to reliable internet and technology resources. 

It is commonly assumed that underfunded schools in spend less on educational technology. This project investigates whether funding alone explains this phenomenon. 

Using multiple public datasets—including the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA), and Washington State School Building Technology Survey, I analyzed technology spending, internet usage, and broadband infrastructure to better understand the underlying causes of the digital divide.

## Research Question

> Is the digital divide primarily a funding issue, or are infrastructure and geographic barriers the larger contributors to unequal technology access?

## Methodology

The project involved:

- Cleaning multiple government datasets
- Filtering school-level technology spending information
- Comparing spending across urban, suburban, town, and rural locales
- Visualizing internet usage trends from 1998–2023
- Examining classroom bandwidth distributions
- Interpreting findings using publicly available research

# Key findings 
### Rural schools spend more on technology
- Although it tends to be assumed that rural schools receive less funding towards technology due to them still having unstable access towards technology, it is actually reported that they receive the highest average per-pupil technology spending, and despite the increased spending, rural schools continue to experience lower internet access. This leads towards more speculations on why the issue has remained an ongoing issue. After conducting further research on the subject, I believe that there have been geographic limitations, and infrastructure remain the primary challenges that affect the rural communities.
